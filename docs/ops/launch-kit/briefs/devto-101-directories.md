# Brief: dev.to data article — what we learned submitting to 101 directories

Written by the maker of AgentoolRank (agentoolrank.com), itself a directory of open-source AI agent tools ranked by live GitHub activity. Say this once near the top. We also run two other small products; the submissions below were made for all three between 2026-09-29 and 2026-10-02, by hand and with browser automation, one submission per site per product, no captcha solving services.

## Facts (only these numbers; do not invent others)
Dataset: 101 directory sites where at least one of our three products went through the submission flow. Facts come from our own notes, last verified 2026-10-02.
- Outcomes: 66 submitted or already listed; 19 we opened and then skipped (rules or fit); 9 did not go through on the first try and are queued for a retry; 5 stopped at a visible captcha (we do not solve captchas); 2 stopped because the free tier required a badge we chose not to place.
- Free is common but rarely free of strings: 81 of 101 have a free option. Of the 101, 56 put free submissions in a review queue, 14 require you to display their badge, 12 require a backlink to them, 1 requires an X post.
- Queues are long: Viesearch shows 1,200+ queued submissions and says its free tier rejects 82%; SaaSHub's free no-login form can take up to 32 days; TinyLaunch scheduled a free launch for 2026-11-02.
- Paid upgrades: 39 sites showed a price; the median entry price was $12.
- Link type: we only recorded rel attributes on 30 sites, usually when something looked off, so this is not a random sample. Of those 30: 23 nofollow, 6 dofollow, 1 ugc. Examples: AlternativeTo listings are nofollow; LaunchBoosts free-plan links are nofollow; make.rs profile links use rel="ugc nofollow"; AI Agents Directory free links are nofollow and dofollow starts at $49.
- Human steps: 30 sites needed someone to open an inbox (verification link, magic link or code), 19 offered or required Google login, 8 had a captcha a person had to complete.
- Small traps we hit: FutureTools shows a newsletter popup you must dismiss with "No thanks" before the form works; SaaSHub loses your management access if you close the browser before claiming the listing; Viesearch's confirmation email can land in spam and you then have to click "Join the Waiting List" and "Wait in Line"; Findly.tools can delist you if the footer badge is removed later.
- Success signals matter for automation: 75 of the 101 sites showed a clear confirmation (e.g. FutureTools "Tool Submitted!"); the rest gave no clear signal, so you have to check later.

## Practical advice the article may give (derived from the facts)
- Budget time for inbox steps and captchas; a fully unattended agent will stall on about a third of sites.
- Read the free tier's conditions before filling the form (badge, backlink, queue).
- Check the rel attribute on a live listing before counting it as a dofollow backlink.
- Keep a log per site (date, result, what blocked you) so you never submit twice and can follow up.
- Do not expect traffic from directories; most value, if any, is links and discoverability. (We saw very little referral traffic so far; do not quote a number.)
